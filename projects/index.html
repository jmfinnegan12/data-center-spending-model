Largest projects

US data center capacity under construction: 37.7 GW (Cushman & Wakefield, Americas, first half of 2026),1 more than 66 GW (JLL, North America, mid-2026),2 about 80 GW (Wood Mackenzie, US, second quarter of 2026).3 By capacity, these projects are roughly a quarter to three fifths of what is being built, and less at any one time, since part of their planned capacity has not started. Census counts private projects of every size.

Confirmed Estimated Range

Capacity is as each source states it: IT load for some projects, total facility power for others, which runs higher. Owner: who owns the buildings. Builder: general contractor. Customer: who uses the capacity. Confirmed: stated by the owner, builder, customer or a government record. Estimated: a third party. Census counts buildings, power and cooling, not chips and servers, so an investment figure that includes them overstates construction. Latest news: ↑ expanding or ahead, ↓ cut or delayed, ↕ mixed.

Signals before each release

Contractor and supplier results, company comments, project news and policy, each counted against the first release it came before. Up: faster than the base rate.

Signals leaned up before every release, and five of six misses were up: no better than guessing slightly above the base rate each month.

I think these signals are better suited to serve as major regime change signals: a major reversal, or a big acceleration given large news or massive outlook changes. They are less useful for marginal adjustments. I rely most heavily on the base rate, and the marginal adjustment should be exactly that, marginal.

Signals for October

The Dodge index's October decline and Microsoft's pullback are the only relevant down signals; each adds marginal downward pressure.

Strength and source quality are subjective.

To watch

  • So little has happened in the Rocky Mountain West, which probably has the most available empty space for these projects, with lots of federal land. It could be a frontier for the next step up in new project starts.4
