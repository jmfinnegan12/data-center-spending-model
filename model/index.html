Base rate

The base rate is simply the prior month adjusted by the rate of change. We get the base rate, then analyze the current state and figure out if, and how, to adjust it.

Only the first published figure counts.4 Census raises most months after first publishing them,2 so the rate of change is measured from first print to first print.5

Why these numbers
Change carried forward from September
The base rate is the prior month adjusted by the rate of change. Five ways of computing it were tested month by month against first prints; the last month's change missed least and had the smallest worst miss, so it is the one used. Adding the change in the change made the misses larger.
Adjustment for the current state
Not yet set.
Standard deviation
A placeholder, not yet chosen. The standard deviation of the base rate's misses is $2.5B on first prints from March to September without May, $4.4B on Census's current figures over the last 12 months, and $7.2B over the last 24.2
Skewness and kurtosis
Not yet set: 0 and 3, the normal distribution.
Assumptions
  • The outcome follows a sinh-arcsinh distribution with the input mean, standard deviation, skewness and kurtosis.7 Some pairs of high skewness and low kurtosis cannot be reached; the readout above the chart shows the values used.
  • October follows the same rule as the months before it.
  • The December 1 release carries no yearly revision. Census makes that each July.6
Weak points
  • Few first prints. The backtest has seven months, one of them distorted by the yearly revision.
  • Most of the figure is estimated, not reported. In 2025, 78% of private nonresidential dollars were filled in by Census for projects that had not reported. No rate is published for data centers alone.3
  • No published error for the data center line.
What would change the forecast
  • First-hand reports of a large project starting, pausing or finishing.
  • A change to Census's methods or to the release date.
Open questions
  • How much of the figure comes from a few very large projects?
  • Can project-level tracking predict which way the base rate will miss?
  • Why has Census raised every recent month after first publishing it?
